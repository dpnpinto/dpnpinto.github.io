Nostalgic & Punchy: Back from the Future: Learning Linux on a 2008 Magalhães

Posted on November 22, 2026 at 23:00 -01

What is this Magalhães

The Magalhães (named after the famous 16th-century Portuguese explorer Ferdinand Magellan, or Fernão de Magalhães) was a rugged, low-cost children's laptop that became a cultural phenomenon in Portugal in the late 2000s. Origins and Purpose Launched in September 2008 under the Portuguese government's e-escolinha initiative (part of the broader Technological Plan for Education), the laptop was designed to bridge the digital divide and put a personal computer into the hands of every primary school student (1st to 4th grade).

Legacy and International Export

While subsequent studies noted that the laptops were often underutilized inside actual classrooms due to a lack of teacher training and digital infrastructure, the Magalhães succeeded in bringing internet access and computing into hundreds of thousands of Portuguese homes for the very first time. Its rugged design also became a Portuguese export, with hundreds of thousands of units shipped to countries like Venezuela, Bolivia, and Argentina.

What I'm doing with it

Today, more than 18 years after its debut, that iconic little machine is far from obsolete. I am actually using a Magalhães right now to navigate the Linux and proving that this classic piece of Portuguese tech still gets the job done!
